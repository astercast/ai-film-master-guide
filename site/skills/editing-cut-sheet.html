Guide / Skills / The Path, stage 6

Run the cut sheet

Gets you: You will have a working cut sheet (the Cut Sheet page) that inspects every shot in your edit and tells you when the picture is ready to lock.

What this is
The editing chapters as a working cut sheet: the five pre-lock gates, every shot logged with its duration, three inspection passes, finding, and route, and the picture-lock verdict from the chapters' rules.
First step
Work the five pre-lock gates in order: selects discipline held (2 to 4 takes per shot, one keeper, alts in a separate bin); generation receipts kept for every kept take (model, prompt, seed, date); takes named like an editor (scene, shot, version, verdict); cut to sound early (temp score in, J/L-cuts placed); lock order respected (picture before grade and mix, upscale last).
You will need
Shot list in cut order with durations; Selects bin (keeps only) with generation receipts; The temp score or sound bed the cut was made against; Defect notes from the inspection pass
Time
About 4 minutes for a first run-through (6 steps; estimated from the procedure itself, your model’s generation time is extra).
Level
Beginner, made to be usable on a first project.
Before this
Best after the skill before it on the Path (skill 238 of 241: Deliver a trailer master), but everything it needs is listed below, and nothing else is assumed.
Proof: the worked example
Worked example on the sheet (cut.html): 'The Night Bus' across 6 shots, 5 clean, the rain stop's hand shot on the repair ladder with one failed attempt, reads 'Hold picture lock' until the repair lands, then 'Picture lock clean'.

Use when

In the edit, from the first assembly to picture lock, and at every lock review after, until the sheet reads clean and survives the 24-hour rule.

Inputs

  • Shot list in cut order with durations
  • Selects bin (keeps only) with generation receipts
  • The temp score or sound bed the cut was made against
  • Defect notes from the inspection pass

Procedure

  1. Work the five pre-lock gates in order: selects discipline held (2 to 4 takes per shot, one keeper, alts in a separate bin); generation receipts kept for every kept take (model, prompt, seed, date); takes named like an editor (scene, shot, version, verdict); cut to sound early (temp score in, J/L-cuts placed); lock order respected (picture before grade and mix, upscale last).
  2. Log every shot with its duration, its three inspection passes, story, background scan, motion at half speed, and the frame-by-frame check any hold over five seconds owes at its start, middle, and end.
  3. Record the finding and the route honestly: no defect, local (bad frames, one element, jitter), or structural (wrong action, wrong composition, identity drift); in the cut, in repair on the ladder, sent back to regenerate, or cut from the film.
  4. Respect the mechanical rules: a hold over five seconds without the frame check reads uninspected; a structural defect routed anywhere but regeneration reads misrouted, repair is for local defects under ~30 minutes; two failed repairs strike a shot out under the two-strike rule.
  5. Work the fix-it ladder bottom-up and stop at the first rung that works: cut around it, re-time, stabilize, composite the fix, grain to unify. Never repair what you can re-prompt, a prompt fix compounds across every future generation.
  6. Close with the chapters' QC: the mute test (the cut plays silent; unmuted, nothing fights the dialogue), the 24-hour rule (lock it, sleep, watch fresh), and the watch on three screens (phone, laptop, TV) before delivery.

Worked example

Worked example on the sheet (cut.html): 'The Night Bus' across 6 shots, 5 clean, the rain stop's hand shot on the repair ladder with one failed attempt, reads 'Hold picture lock' until the repair lands, then 'Picture lock clean'.

Pitfalls

  • Cutting AI footage silent and metronomic, cut to the temp score early; synthetic footage has no rest state, so rhythm is doing more work than you think.
  • Holding an AI shot past five seconds because it looks good at full speed, drift accumulates at the start, middle, and end; frame-check it or trim it.
  • Repairing a structural defect for an hour because the generation was expensive, the two-strike rule exists for exactly that sunk cost.
