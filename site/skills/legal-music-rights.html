Clear AI music rights properly

Gets you: You will know what your AI music licence really lets you do, sell, stream, monetise, before a claim teaches you the hard way.

What this is
A commercial license is not copyright, know what you actually hold.
First step
Generate only while on a paid commercial tier (Suno Pro, Udio Pro, AIVA, Soundraw), keep subscription receipts, generation dates, download records.
You will need
Every music/voice/SFX track's source
Time
About 3 minutes for a first run-through (5 steps; estimated from the procedure itself, your model’s generation time is extra).
Level
Beginner, made to be usable on a first project.
Before this
Best after the skill before it on the Path (skill 242 of 257: Verify likeness clearance), but everything it needs is listed below, and nothing else is assumed.
Proof: the worked example
Festival entry: all-Soundraw score, receipts + cue sheet, paperwork passed first review.

Use when

Before locking any AI-generated music into the film.

Inputs

  • Every music/voice/SFX track's source

Procedure

  1. Generate only while on a paid commercial tier (Suno Pro, Udio Pro, AIVA, Soundraw), keep subscription receipts, generation dates, download records.
  2. Understand the gap: the platform grants a contractual right to use; it cannot grant copyright if none vests (US Copyright Office: no copyright without human authorship). Distributors and insurers ask for proof of rights, an AI track is a thin file.
  3. For maximum defensibility: human-arranged tools (AIVA Pro), royalty-free libraries (Soundraw), or commissioned humans.
  4. Build the music cue sheet: every track's source, tier, generation date, rights grant.
  5. Check the tool's current terms at spend time, e.g., Udio downloads were disabled platform-wide, a commercial right with no export.

Worked example

Festival entry: all-Soundraw score, receipts + cue sheet, paperwork passed first review.

Pitfalls

  • Assuming 'commercial license' = 'I own it', the E&O questionnaire will find the gap.
