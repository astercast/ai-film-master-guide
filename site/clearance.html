The cutting room

Script clearance.

Every name, place, business and object in the script, researched against the real world — and proved in a file. The ten steps, your production's items, a recorded decision for every flag, and the gate the insurer and the distributor both check: no completed clearance, no cover, no distribution.

The ten steps — the file itself

Script clearance is the layer under every other rights question in this guide: what may lawfully appear in the work at all. The ten steps below are the ten rules from research cycle 81 (rules 1358–1367), each with what the document is and when it is required; the full chapter is Script Clearance: The Report, the Chain, and the Gate. Tick a step when the production has actually done it — the desk counts paper, not intentions. Not legal advice: this is the file a clearance researcher and an entertainment attorney work from.

1

The clearance report

Rule 1358 · Before the shoot

The final shooting script, researched by a professional clearance researcher: every character name, place, address and business checked against its real-world commonality, and the permission contact named for every referenced product, copyright and trademark.

When: Filed before production, on the final shooting script.

2

The annotation guide

Rule 1359 · Before the shoot

The writer labels every character, place and event fictional, real or composite; the work's origins and any similar submission's independent path are documented, and true-life sources are recorded as primary or secondary.

When: At the start — it is the clearance review's input, not its output.

3

Researched names, permitted numbers

Rule 1360 · Before the shoot

A fictional name, business or address that coincides with a real, identifiable person or entity is changed or released before use — coincidence is a defect, not a defence — and only permitted telephone and plate numbers ever appear.

When: With the report, before any flagged element is shot or rendered.

4

Chain of title + copyright report

Rule 1361 · Before the shoot

Ownership proven as an unbroken filed record — origin plus every transfer, work-for-hire or option tested, contributors without releases found, encumbrances quitclaimed — verified by a copyright report covering domestic, foreign and renewal rights.

When: From acquisition onward, and re-run by the distributor at delivery.

5

The title report

Rule 1362 · Before the shoot

The title is researched before final selection; the report lists prior same and similar uses, a conflict changes the title, and the report is filed for the distributors and insurers who require it.

When: Before the title is final — and again when the title is chosen at the end of the process.

6

Release coverage

Rule 1363 · During production

Written releases cover every recognizably depicted person — thinly disguised and deceased included, the latter via representatives or heirs — every performer, recognizable extra and crew member, and every distinctive location.

When: As each person, performance and location enters the production.

7

The featured-object caution

Rule 1364 · During production

Props, products, artwork and photographs are cleared one by one under an abundance of caution — prominence in the final form is unknowable in advance, and supplied or previously used items are never assumed already cleared.

When: Item by item, as dressing, props and artwork are placed.

8

The living report

Rule 1365 · During production

The clearance report is updated whenever new material enters — script versions, boards, schedules, the shoot, post-production — and clearance monitoring runs at all stages, not as a single pass at the start.

When: Continuously, from inception through final cut.

9

The recorded decision

Rule 1366 · Closing the file

Every flagged item is closed by a recorded decision — license it, substitute it, change the way it is used, or expressly assume the risk — and an undecided flagged item blocks the element it flags.

When: Per flagged item, before the element it flags can ship.

10

The final check + the gate

Rule 1367 · Closing the file

Before exhibition the finished work is reviewed against the clearance file for what the script never contained, trailers and publicity receive their own clearance analysis, and the completed file is the gate — no completed clearance, no cover and no distribution.

When: Before exhibition, on the finished film — not the script.

Steps complete: 0 of 10 — the file is not complete until every step is done.

Your clearance file

Log the production's items one by one — every name, place, business, person, location and featured object the research touches. A flagged item closes only with a recorded decision (license, substitute, change the use, or expressly assume the risk); an undecided flag holds the gate.
