Story Structure for AI Short Films

The short-film engine

A 3–5 minute AI film needs exactly three things:

  1. One want — the protagonist wants something concrete, visible on screen
  2. One obstacle — something specific stands in the way
  3. One turn — the moment everything changes

Most AI-film losers are tech demos with no second act: pretty shots, no turn. Judges (especially Oscar-caliber ones) punish this first.

The judged-short template

  • Cold open (0:00–0:30): drop into the world mid-action. No setup, no title cards explaining the premise.
  • Escalation (0:30–2:30): the want meets the obstacle, pressure builds. Each shot raises the stakes or deepens the world.
  • Reversal (2:30–3:30): the turn — the plan fails, the truth lands, the choice gets made.
  • Button (3:30–5:00): one final image that reframes everything before it. End on the image, not on explanation.

Writing for the model

Design scenes around what AI renders well; write away from what it doesn't.

Lean into: single subjects, strong silhouettes, weather and atmosphere, landscapes, slow deliberate motion, objects and environments, stylized looks.

Write around: complex hand action, crowd choreography, intricate dialogue scenes, fast fight choreography, text/signage in frame, real people or voices (festival rules forbid them anyway).

The constraint is the style: the best AI films don't hide the medium — they pick stories the medium tells better than live action could (impossible scale, dream logic, metamorphosis).

Scene design checklist (per scene)

  • One clear want visible in the frame
  • One action per shot, five seconds or less each
  • The turn is a visual event, not dialogue
  • Every scene either raises stakes or deepens world — never both vaguely, never neither
  • The ending image is planned before the first shot is generated
