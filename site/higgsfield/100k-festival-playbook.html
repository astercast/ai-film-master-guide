The $100K Festival Playbook

The "$100,000 prize" is real — it's the 3rd-place and Audience Choice tier of the Higgsfield Global Film Festival, a $1,000,000 competition. This chapter is the playbook for it.

The competition
Total prize pool$1,000,000 across 14 winners
1st place$500,000
2nd place$200,000
3rd place$100,000
Audience Choice$100,000
Honorable Mention ×10$10,000 each
Runtime3–5 minutes
EligibilityHiggsfield subscribers, 18+, solo or teams up to 4, worldwide

Jury: Edwin Catmull (Pixar co-founder, five-time Oscar winner) and Phedon Papamichael (two-time Oscar-nominated cinematographer, Ford v Ferrari) among others — an Oscar-caliber panel, not a marketing jury.

Timeline (2026 edition): submissions opened early August, deadline mid-September 2026; screening and shortlist through early October; jury review early-mid October; winners announced end of October 2026.

The rules that shape your production

  • Minimum viable submission: final video with Higgsfield's watermark and packshot, plus a public post of the same video on Instagram, YouTube, X, or Reddit (must stay public, no login wall — a private account disqualifies you).
  • Music, voice, and sound design must be AI-generated, uploaded into the submission project, with rights held by the creator.
  • No real person's face or voice — not even your own.
  • One film gets one prize; one participant/team gets one prize across all entries. Prizes don't stack.
  • Any genre, any story.

Strategy notes

  • The honorable-mention math: 10 shots at $10,000 is a different bet than one shot at $500,000. Ten finalists get paid — aim for the top fourteen, not just the top spot.
  • The jury reads like a real film festival. Catmull and Papamichael reward story, cinematography, and craft — not prompt cleverness. Direct for them, not for AI Twitter.
  • Sound is judged. AI-generated music/voice/sound is mandatory — a silent film or stock-music film is leaving points on the table by rule, not just taste.
  • Higgsfield open-sourced three of its own AI films with every prompt, camera setting, and asset published. Study those before spending a credit.

What won before: the Action Contest

Higgsfield's earlier Action Contest (8,752 submissions, 139 countries) shows what this jury DNA rewards:

  • 1st ($150,000): "GRANDMA vs WASP" — Muhannad Nassar & Simon Meyer. Never met in person; async workflow across time zones in Cinema Studio. Action short, decentralized production.
  • 2nd ($100,000): "CUPID" — Shestak Nikolay, actor/filmmaker. Concepts that would need prohibitive traditional budgets — AI used for scale, not novelty.
  • 3rd ($50,000): "SCRATCH" — Ash & Aram Gevorkyan, made in five days. Director + 3D/VFX generalist duo; production value so high audiences mistook it for a studio trailer.
  • 20 honorable mentions at $10,000 each.

Pattern: experienced filmmakers using AI for impossible scale, tight teams, real craft — not tech demos.

Submission checklist

  • 3–5 minute final cut, watermark + packshot included
  • All music/voice/sound AI-generated, files in the submission project, rights cleared
  • No real faces or voices anywhere in the film
  • Public social post live (Instagram/YouTube/X/Reddit), account public
  • Submitted before the deadline in your timezone
  • Team of 4 or fewer, all 18+, active subscription
