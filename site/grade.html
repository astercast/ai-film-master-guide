The cutting room

Grade sheet.

Every generation arrives wearing its own grade. The five gates before the grade, every scene logged with its hero reference, its source, and its five matching steps, and the grade verdict from the grading chapter's own rules: no reference means unmatched, and unfinished steps hold the grade.

The five gates, before the grade

Every AI generation arrives with its own built-in grade, the model's idea of what "cinematic" looks like, baked into the pixels. On a multi-shot production that is 40 different colorists who never met; the colorist's job is unification. The five gates below are the grading chapter's own discipline before any scene is touched; the full chapter is Color Grading AI Footage. Tick a gate when the production has actually done it, the sheet counts what is written down, not intentions.

1

Every element assigned: plate or grade

Pre-grade gate

Decide per element which place its color lives. What you bake into the plate, lens character, palette direction, texture, time of day, you don't have to fix; anything the video model keeps dropping belongs in the plate, and anything that must move across the whole film belongs in the grade.

2

Hero reference chosen for every scene

Pre-grade gate

Pick one hero shot per scene as the reference and match every other shot to it, scopes, not eyes. Do the unification pass over the location set before generating where possible: it is cheaper than discovering the mismatch in the grade.

3

One show look locked, scene variations only

Pre-grade gate

One look for the film, with scene-level variations, not a new look per scene. Work in passes: primary (whole image), then secondaries (skin, sky, key objects), then texture. Restraint wins: halation and bloom flatter AI highlights, which tend to clip harshly.

4

Grain philosophy decided, one grain, applied last

Pre-grade gate

One grain for the film, applied at the end, over everything, it is the final unifier. Decide it before the first scene is graded, not after the last one looks different from the rest.

5

Pre-grade masters archived

Pre-grade gate

Keep the pre-grade masters. When the festival asks for a revision, you re-grade, you don't re-generate. Archive the masters with the project, at delivery resolution, before the creative look goes on.

Gates complete: 0 of 5, the gates are the discipline before any scene is judged; finish them first.

Your grade sheet

Log every scene with its hero reference (one shot per scene that every other shot is matched to), its source (one model, or several, different models carry different house grades, and mixed scenes unify harder), and the five matching steps in order: exposure and white balance, contrast curve, saturation, then the de-plasticize texture pass (fine grain, a whisper of chromatic aberration, soften, never sharpen), skin last and on a big screen. Mark each scene as it stands today: unified, drifting, or unmatched. Two rules are mechanical: a scene with no hero reference reads unmatched whatever you claim, because unification is matching to a reference; and a scene claimed unified with steps unfinished reads drifting, because the creative look never goes on before the match. The sheet opens on a worked example, the same small AI short the Ledger and the Cue Sheet track, graded instead of seen or heard, with the rain stop shot across two models and its saturation unmatched, so the first verdict you see is the honest hold.

Hold the grade, 1 scene is drifting: the matching steps are unfinished. Finish the unification pass first (exposure and white balance, contrast curve, saturation, scopes, not eyes), then the de-plasticize stack in order, skin last; a creative look laid over an unmatched scene has to be laid again.
