Continuity ledger.

The five locks — before any generation

The consistency chapter opens with the sentence every AI filmmaker learns the expensive way: this is where most festival entries die. The five locks below are the chapter's own pre-generation checklist; the full chapter is Character Consistency. Tick a lock when the production has actually done it — the ledger counts what is written down, not intentions.

1

Identity system chosen and trained

Pre-generation lock

The identity stack, ranked by reliability: trained identity (Soul ID) first, then the character sheet as reference, then a per-generation reference upload. Text description alone is the weakest — never rely on prose to hold a face across 50 shots.

2

Wardrobe description locked, verbatim

Pre-generation lock

Write the wardrobe once, in exact language, and paste it verbatim into every prompt. A mid-film wardrobe change is a new locked description — never improvised per shot. Props get the same treatment: one description, reused exactly.

3

Location master stills approved

Pre-generation lock

Generate and approve a master still of each location first. Every shot in that location starts from it or references it.

4

Light direction + time of day locked per scene

Pre-generation lock

Lock time of day and light direction per scene — “golden-hour side light from frame left” in every prompt of the scene. Pick the era and colour palette up front; don't grade shot-by-shot and hope.

5

Start frame is real, never invented

Pre-generation lock

Every clip starts from the last frame of the previous approved clip, or from the approved-frame library. Never fabricate a start frame. One human per start frame on character-led work — crowded frames multiply failure modes.

Checklist complete: 0 of 5 — the checklist is the gate before any generation; finish it first.

Your continuity ledger

Log every lock the film depends on — a face, a wardrobe line pasted verbatim, a hero prop, a location master still, the light of a scene, the era and grade. Mark each one as it stands today: locked, drifted, or broken. The verdict follows the chapter's rejection rules: a clip fails if the face drifts, the wardrobe changes, or the light flips — and failed clips don't chain, they get re-prompted. The ledger opens on a worked example: a small AI short with one wardrobe lock drifted, so the first verdict you see is the honest hold.

Hold — 1 lock has drifted off the locked wording or reference. Re-lock before the next generation: drift compounds shot over shot, and the next clip chains from this one.
