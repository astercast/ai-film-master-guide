Cut sheet.

Synthetic footage never sits still, so the cut is where the film is made. The five gates before picture lock, every shot logged with its duration, its three inspection passes, its finding, and its route, and the lock verdict from the editing chapters' own rules: the 5-second rule, the structural rule, the two-strike rule.

The five gates — before picture lock

Synthetic footage has no rest state: a still AI room shimmers, breathes, and drifts at the pixel level, so the cut is where the film is actually made. The five gates below are the editing chapters' own discipline before any shot is judged; the full chapters are Editing AI Footage and Editing Pipeline. Tick a gate when the production has actually done it — the sheet counts what is written down, not intentions.

1

Selects discipline held: 2–4 takes, one keeper

Pre-lock gate

Generate 2–4 takes per shot and keep the best one — the first generation is a draft, and takes 2–4 are where the model settles. Past four takes you are usually prompting the wrong thing: go back to the prompt, not the generate button. Only keeps enter the edit; alts live in a separate bin.

2

Generation receipts kept for every kept take

Pre-lock gate

For every take you keep, record the model, the prompt (or prompt ID), the seed if the tool exposes one, and the generation date. When a shot needs a re-render three weeks later, the receipt is the difference between matching it and starting over.

3

Takes named like an editor, not a prompter

Pre-lock gate

Scene, shot, version, verdict: sc03_sh02_v3_keep.mov beats kling_dreamy_final_FINAL2.mp4. The verdict suffix (_keep, _alt, _fix) is the whole system — at 2am you should be able to read the selects bin without opening anything.

4

Cut to sound early: temp score in, J/L-cuts placed

Pre-lock gate

Even a rough music bed gives synthetic footage a pulse to cut against; editors who cut AI footage silent tend to cut it metronomically. Let audio lead or lag the picture cut — J-cuts and L-cuts are free professionalism, and they cost nothing.

5

Lock order respected: picture before grade and mix, upscale last

Pre-lock gate

Picture lock comes before color and mix — the grade and the cue sheet both start from the locked cut. One grain goes over the locked timeline, and the upscale is of the final timeline only: never upscale individual clips and re-edit; you pay for pixels you cut.

Gates complete: 0 of 5 — the gates are the discipline before any shot is judged; finish them first.

Your cut sheet

Log every shot in the cut with its duration, its three inspection passes (the story pass; the background scan — hands at frame edges, background faces, signs, reflections; the motion pass at half speed, where micro-jitter and swimming cloth show), and the frame-by-frame check any hold over five seconds owes at its start, middle, and end. Record what the inspection found — no defect, a local defect (bad frames, one element, jitter), or a structural one (wrong action, wrong composition, identity drift) — and the shot's route: in the cut, in repair on the fix-it ladder (cut around it, re-time, stabilize, composite, grain — bottom-up, stop at the first rung that works), sent back to regenerate, or cut from the film. Three rules are mechanical: a hold over five seconds without the frame check reads uninspected; a structural defect routed anywhere but regeneration reads misrouted; and a repair with two failed attempts reads struck — the two-strike rule sends it back. The sheet opens on a worked example — the same small AI short the Ledger, the Cue Sheet, and the Grade Sheet track, cut instead of seen, heard, or graded — with the rain stop's hand shot on the repair ladder, so the first verdict you see is the honest hold.

Hold picture lock — 1 shot is not ready: a repair still on the ladder, a shot sent back for regeneration, an inspection unfinished, or a local defect still carried in the cut. Work the ladder bottom-up — cut around it, re-time, stabilize, composite — and stop at the first rung that works.
