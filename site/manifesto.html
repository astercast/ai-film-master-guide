The manifesto

This is not a course.

No cohort. No certificate. No $2,000 upsell. This is a living reference for people making films with AI, and it has opinions.

What this is

A reference manual you keep open while you work, twenty-six chapters on craft, platforms, economics, and pipelines, plus the tools (budget calculator, prompt workshop, model quiz, teardowns) and the machine-readable API underneath. It is updated as the field moves: the changelog is dated, the model data is versioned, and corrections are printed, not buried.

What it costs

Nothing. Courses have their place, structure, community, and feedback from a working filmmaker are genuinely worth paying for, and this guide does not replace them. This is the other thing: the manual their graduates keep open after the cohort ends. The reference layer, free, forever, with no account.

Everything is sourced

Every claim in this guide carries a URL, a confidence flag, or an explicit unknown. When we get something wrong, we print the correction, this guide once repeated a Cannes premiere claim for ONEIRIC that didn't hold up; the correction is in the text, not in a quiet edit. Trust is the whole product. A reference that hides its mistakes is marketing.

What it won't do

  • No hype. AI will not replace filmmakers; it replaced the render farm. The guide says what the tools actually do.
  • No tool worship. Craft over buttons. The prompting chapters teach directing; the platform chapters are comparisons, not loyalty.
  • No secrets. There is no "one prompt trick." There is story structure, shot discipline, and credit math, unglamorous and learnable.
  • No vendor lock-in. Eighteen models, compared honestly, weaknesses included. The quiz recommends from data, not sponsorship.
  • No invented facts. Unknowns are labeled as unknowns. If a budget, timeline, or technique couldn't be verified, it isn't in here.

How to use it

Ten minutes: take the model quiz, fill a template in the prompt workshop, run your numbers through the budget calculator. You'll know what to shoot with, what to write, and what it costs.

Ten hours: pick a learning path, first film, competition-ready craft, animated, photoreal, long-form. Tick the steps off; your progress is saved in this browser.

As a reference: press Ctrl+K (or ⌘K) anywhere and search. Print any chapter or field guide for the set.

Who it's for

People making films with AI who want craft, not content. If you want the fastest path to a viral clip, there are cheaper guides. If you want to direct, to learn why a shot works, what a performance needs, and what a minute of footage actually costs, this is yours.
