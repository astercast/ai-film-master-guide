The cutting room

Cue sheet.

Sound is half the picture. The five gates before the mix, every scene logged with its three layers, bed, foley, accents, and its dialogue route, and the mix verdict from the sound chapter's own rules: no bed means dry, and a thin scene holds the mix.

The five gates, before the mix

The sound chapter opens with the claim every AI filmmaker pays to learn: sound is half the picture, and finished films are designed, not slapped together. The five gates below are the chapter's own pre-mix discipline; the full chapter is Sound, Music & Audio. Tick a gate when the production has actually done it, the sheet counts what is written down, not intentions.

1

Voice Bible written for every speaking character

Pre-mix gate

Register, timbre, tempo, manner, written per character before generating, and referenced by every generation after. Test the voice on the character's hardest line first, not a demo sentence: a voice that collapses on the hardest scene was never cast.

2

Dialogue route chosen per scene, never mixed mid-scene

Pre-mix gate

Road A, native: the model generates picture plus synced dialogue in one pass, best for short lines and ambient realism. Road B, decoupled: silent clip, separately produced voice, a lip-sync pass that reshapes only the mouth. Rule of thumb: if the words must be exact, take Road B.

3

Score briefed per cue, cut to the locked cut

Pre-mix gate

Brief by emotional function, not genre, genre, tempo, instrumentation, the cue's job, what to avoid, and generate 3 to 5 variants per cue to comp. Temp-track discipline: cut to a temp score first, then generate the final score to the locked cut. Never score a moving target.

4

Stems kept for the score and the voices

Pre-mix gate

Dialogue ducks badly under a full stereo mix; stems let the mix breathe. Keep the score's stems and every voice take separate until the mix is signed off, a bounced stereo pair is a decision you cannot unmake.

5

Generation receipts saved for every AI voice and cue

Pre-mix gate

AI-generated music and voices sit in the murkiest rights territory in the guide. Keep the receipts, prompts, dates, tool, terms version, for every generated voice and cue, and read the rights chapter before distributing anything with AI music in it.

Gates complete: 0 of 5, the gates are the discipline before any scene is judged; finish them first.

Your cue sheet

Log every scene with the three layers the chapter requires, the ambient bed, the foley that makes movement physical, the accents that land the story beats, and the dialogue route chosen for it: decoupled when the words must be exact, native for short ambient lines, never mixed mid-scene. Mark each scene as it stands today: designed, thin, or dry. One rule is mechanical: a scene with no bed reads dry whatever you claim, because the bed runs under everything and never drops to digital silence. The sheet opens on a worked example, the same small AI short the Continuity Ledger tracks, heard instead of seen, with the rain stop's foley not yet laid, so the first verdict you see is the honest hold.

Hold the mix, 1 scene is thin: a layer is missing or rough. Work the build order, dialogue first, then the SFX bed, then music last; a thin scene finished under a full score stays thin.

Sc 1, Depot at night, Mara waits

Decoupled

Bed: depot room tone, rain on the canopy. Foley: boots on wet concrete, bag set down. Accent: the departure-board flip on her line.

State: Designed, built and checked

Sc 2, On the bus, the hardest line

Decoupled

Voice per the Voice Bible (chest register, unhurried, flat warmth), comped from paragraph takes. Bed: engine idle under everything. Accent: ticket punch on the turn.

State: Designed, built and checked

Sc 3, The rain stop

Decoupled

Bed: rain on the shelter roof, road hiss. Accents: bus sigh and door chime laid. Foley not laid yet, her walk to the shelter is still silent under the bed.

State: Thin, a layer missing or rough

Sc 4, Depot office, radio voices

Native

Short native lines only, nothing word-exact in this scene. Bed: office tone, fluorescent hum. Foley: mug, keys, chair. Accent: radio squelch into the reveal.

State: Designed, built and checked

Sc 5, Departure, the button

No dialogue

No dialogue, the scene plays on design alone. Bed: depot tone thinning as the bus pulls out. Foley: steps, door, pull-away. Accent: one brake sigh under the final frame; score enters only after.

State: Designed, built and checked

Take the sheet

The sheet exports as clean markdown: the five gates with their state, every scene with its route, its layers, and its verdict state, and the mix verdict, the file the mix session starts from.
