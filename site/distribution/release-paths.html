Distribution & Release Paths

The film is finished. Now it needs an audience — and AI films face a distribution landscape that's forming in real time. This chapter maps where AI films actually go, what each path demands, and what to verify before you commit. Policies and deals below were read on 2026-10-11; they change fast — re-verify before acting.

The emerging dedicated distributors

AI-native distribution now exists as a category:

  • Escape.AI ("Neo Cinema") — founded by Oscar-winning VFX pioneer John Gaeta. A curated AI-cinema streaming ecosystem distributing via a Play.Works partnership to Roku, Amazon Fire TV, LG and Samsung smart TVs (claimed 400M+ addressable CTV devices). Creators get a 50% net revenue split on syndicated CTV content, plus tipping, channel subscriptions, and digital collectibles — while retaining 100% of underlying IP. (Company release via PR Newswire, Oct 2026)
  • AI Era Corp (OTC: AERA) — runs the uFilm.ai production loop (idea → 100-episode AI series script). On August 28, 2026 it signed an online broadcast-rights licensing agreement worth ~$1.26M for one package of AI-generated series, explicitly retaining AI training rights on every title. Management expects broadcast-rights licensing to generate "several million dollars" per year. (Company release via GlobeNewswire, Aug 2026 — figures are company-reported)
  • iQIYI — the major Chinese streamer's AIGC series The Ferry Man passed RMB 8 million in cumulative revenue-sharing as of September 2026; the first two installments recovered production costs within their first week. Third installment debuted October 1, 2026. (Announcement via PR Newswire, Oct 2026 — note: revenue-sharing, not gross revenue)

What this means: there is now a real, if young, market for AI-native content — weighted toward series and CTV, with IP retention as the standard deal term. Company-reported figures are marketing; treat them as direction, not data.

YouTube: the default self-release

The Cully Hill Boys (110-minute AI feature) premiered August 5, 2026 and streams free on YouTube, with all prompts and assets open-sourced — the current template for AI-feature release.

YouTube monetization of AI content (2026 rules): disclosure and "inauthentic content" are separate tracks. Disclosure is a transparency requirement, not a penalty — it does not by itself limit reach or monetization. The monetization risk is the mass-produced/repetitive content rule: channels publishing many near-identical AI videos with weak variation can be treated as inauthentic. Reviewers assess the channel as a whole — main theme, most-viewed, newest, highest-watch-time videos, titles, thumbnails, About section. Channels using AI personas as fake experts on health, legal, finance, or politics cannot monetize. (Third-party explainers of YouTube policy — read YouTube's own Help pages before relying on this.)

Practical read: one authored film on a real channel is fine. A channel farming fifty near-identical AI shorts is not.

Traditional indie paths still apply

The standard options — limited theatrical, streaming deal, TVOD/EST, self-distribution (Vimeo OTT, own site), hybrid — all work for AI films. Evaluate distributors on: track record with similar films, minimum guarantee, deductible expenses, territories, marketing plan, reporting transparency, rights granted and term.

Distributor deliverables for an acquired film: DCP, ProRes masters, M&E track (music & effects, no dialogue — for international), closed captions/subtitles, key art in multiple sizes, trailer, press kit, chain of title documents, E&O insurance certificate, music cue sheet, photo releases. (Tribeca Film Institute Distribution 101 — industry-standard practice.)

Honest gap: as of October 2026, no verified report exists of a major Western streamer (Netflix/Disney+/Max) acquiring an AI-generated feature outright. Streamers use AI inside productions (Netflix reportedly used AI in 300 of 1,000 titles in 2026) — but they are not yet buying AI films as AI films. Do not plan around a streamer acquisition that hasn't happened yet.

Release checklist

  • Disclosure set correctly on every platform (see legal/rights-and-disclosure.md)
  • Deliverables mastered: DCP/ProRes, M&E track, captions, key art, trailer, press kit
  • Chain of title + E&O in hand before any distributor conversation
  • Channel/account health checked (no inauthentic-content risk factors)
  • Release assets (prompts, behind-the-scenes) prepared if going the open-source route
