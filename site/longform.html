The feature floor

Longform sheet.

Long films are won with systems, not prompts. The five gates, before feature lock

Short films are won with great prompts; long films are won with systems. The five gates below are the long-form chapter's own discipline before any scene is judged; the full chapter is Long-Form Pipelines: Making AI Films That Survive 90 Minutes. Tick a gate when the production has actually done it, the sheet counts what is written down, not intentions.

1

Asset registry complete before shot one

Pre-lock gate

Every character, location, and prop named, versioned, and locked in the registry before the first shot is generated. State changes (scar, haircut, dirt, wardrobe) get a new @-tag, state is an asset, never an overwrite.

2

Every scene stress-tested and storyboarded

Pre-lock gate

Goal, Obstacle, Tactic, Reversal, Value Shift, with a verdict and fixes, scene by scene, then storyboarded step by step. Only on the storyboard does it become clear how good a scene really is; a weak scene's cost is discovered after it is generated.

3

Architecture chosen: chaining, or sequence assembly with a wrangler

Pre-lock gate

Solo or small team: scene-by-scene chaining, each new scene's reference is the previous scene's finished video. Team of five-plus: sequence assembly, but only if someone owns the registry full-time; without a wrangler, parallel generation produces parallel continuities.

4

Dialogue architecture chosen per scene, not per film

Pre-lock gate

Native speech, human-recorded and AI-converted, or decoupled TTS, chosen per scene. Budget the continuity and re-roll effort on ordinary rooms, not spectacle: the one-room dialogue scene is the hardest task in the record, harder than the space battle.

5

Lock discipline written: base never re-run, plates carry the look, Voice Bible verbatim

Pre-lock gate

The base portrait is never re-run through any model. The look lives in the asset plate, not the video prompt. The Voice Bible is pasted into every generation verbatim, not even a synonym changed. Every prompt is an island: everything re-stated, every time.

Gates complete: 0 of 5, the gates are the discipline before any scene is judged; finish them first.

Your longform sheet

Log every scene with its stress test (Goal, Obstacle, Tactic, Reversal, Value Shift, with a verdict and fixes) done or not, its storyboard done or not, its reference, a video reference (the previous scene's finished video or a previz), a text description only, or none yet, its dialogue route (native speech, human-recorded and AI-converted, decoupled TTS, or no dialogue), its draft tier (720p draft, an upscaled winner, or full-res generation), its re-roll count, and its route: in production, picture-locked, in generation supervision (the scheduled QC/re-generation pass after the rough cut), or cut from the film. Three rules are mechanical: a scene generated without its stress test reads untested; a scene chained off a text description reads drifting, because a text description drifts over generations and a video reference doesn't; and a scene generated at full resolution before its winner is chosen reads wasteful, draft at 720p, upscale winners only. The sheet opens on a worked example, the same fictional production the other sheets track, built long as a 24-minute film, with the depot-office sequence in generation supervision, so the first verdict you see is the honest hold.

Hold feature lock, 1 scene is not ready: still in production, in the scheduled generation-supervision pass, or missing its storyboard or video reference. Drift is cumulative, not linear, a seam left unchecked is paid for in every scene after it.
