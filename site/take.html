The rehearsal room

Take sheet.

Models don't act, direction makes pixels read as acting. The five gates before performance lock, every beat logged with its tactic, its eyeline, its dead-eyes check, its framing, and its route, and the lock verdict from the directing chapter's own rules: the label rule, the three-rehearsal rule, the close-up rule.

The five gates, before performance lock

AI models don't act: they render plausible arrangements of pixels that read as acting when the direction is specific enough, and collapse into mannequin theater when it isn't. The five gates below are the directing chapter's own discipline before any beat is judged; the full chapter is Directing Performance in AI Video. Tick a gate when the production has actually done it, the sheet counts what is written down, not intentions.

1

Every beat rewritten as a playable tactic

Pre-lock gate

Direct, don't describe: replace every emotion label with a task a body can do, scene event, motive, goal, obstacle, tactic. Models can't render 'devastated'; they can render a jaw set and eyes fixed on a door.

2

Eyelines named per beat, every pair of eyes has a job

Pre-lock gate

Dead, glassy eyes are never fixed with lighting, they're fixed by giving the eyes a job. Name the eyeline target per beat; eyes move between targets (avert, hold, search, track) and never stare into the middle distance.

3

Director's read written: shared event + a channel per character

Pre-lock gate

Everyone in the scene lives through the same event, but each character carries it in a different physical channel, grief in her hands, in his jaw, in the kid's feet. Write the event once, then assign each character their channel.

4

Identity locked, the face anchor is never re-run

Pre-lock gate

Consistent identity reads as good acting; a face that drifts 5% reads as a bad performance. Lock the face anchor, never re-run the base portrait, and make state changes (dirt, wounds, tears) masked integrations, not re-generations.

5

Restraint pass done: negative constraints, subtext, under-directed faces

Pre-lock gate

Models default to theatrical. Strip it: negative constraints (no eyebrow raises, no wide-eyed gasps), subtext instead of stated emotion, environment carrying mood, and deliberately under-direct the face where the model finds expression better itself.

Gates complete: 0 of 5, the gates are the discipline before any beat is judged; finish them first.

Your take sheet

Log every performance beat with its direction, a playable tactic (a task a body can do: scene event, motive, goal, obstacle, tactic) or an emotion label (an adjective, which is not direction), its eyeline target named or not, its dead-eyes check (microsaccades, the Duchenne smile, fluctuating asymmetry, pupillary response), its framing and whether that framing sits on an emotional peak, its failed rehearsals, and its route: in rehearsal (720p drafts as takes), Scene-Locked (photograph the performance, don't regenerate it), to be simplified, or hybrid (a real performer at the emotional core). Three rules are mechanical: a beat directed by emotion label reads undirected; a beat still re-rolling after three failed rehearsals reads overdue, simplify the shot, not the actor; and a close-up spent away from an emotional peak reads misspent. The sheet opens on a worked example, the same small AI short the Ledger, the Cue Sheet, the Grade Sheet, and the Cut Sheet track, performed instead of seen, heard, graded, or cut, with the depot-office reaction still in rehearsal, so the first verdict you see is the honest hold.

Hold performance lock, 1 beat is not ready: a take still in rehearsal, a simplification still owed, or the eyeline / dead-eyes check unfinished. Review drafts as rehearsals, does the tactic read, do the eyes have a job, does the turn land in the cut?
