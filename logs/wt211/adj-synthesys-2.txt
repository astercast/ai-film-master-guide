This comparison uses published information from ByteDance, BytePlus and Kling, plus public leaderboards and other people&#x27;s tests, checked on 1 October 2026. We haven&#x27;t run our own side-by-side test yet. Seedance vs Kling is a moving target. Most comparisons you&#x27;ll find test Seedance 2.0 against Kling 3.0. Since then, ByteDance shipped Seedance 2.5, and Kling announced Kling 4.0 for October. This post compares what you can use today, from the makers&#x27; own pages. Most people ask which model is best. The better question is what the clip has to do: run 30 seconds, speak Hindi, or fill a 4K screen. What is the difference between Seedance and Kling? Seedance is ByteDance&#x27;s family of video models, and Kling is Kuaishou&#x27;s. Both make clips with sound from text, images and reference files. Seedance leans on long clips and many reference files. Kling leans on 4K output, shot-by-shot control and a lower price per second on its API. These are the current models: Seedance 2.0 , launched in February 2026 , with cheaper Fast and Mini versions on BytePlus . Seedance 2.5 , launched on 31 July 2026 for clips up to 30 seconds. Its API is now live on BytePlus ModelArk . Kling 3.0 and 3.0 Omni , fully rolled out on 31 January 2026 . Native 4K arrived on 23 April , and the faster Kling 3.0 Turbo on 17 June. Kling 4.0 , which Kling says will launch officially in October . Kling 4.0 Flash opened early to Ultra yearly subscribers on 28 September. How do Seedance and Kling compare on paper? On published specs, Seedance 2.5 makes the longest clips today and takes the most reference files. Kling 3.0 offers native 4K and per-shot control, and its API costs less per second at 1080p. Kling 4.0 closes the length gap at 30 seconds once it fully launches. Kling&#x27;s app plans were showing first-month offers when we checked. Which makes longer clips? Seedance 2.5 does today: up to 30 seconds per run, and it can extend a clip in more rounds. Seedance 2.0 and Kling 3.0 both stop at 15 seconds. Kling 4.0 lists 3 to 30 seconds, but Kling says it launches fully in October. Its 2-minute extension is marked &quot;coming soon&quot;. ByteDance says 2.5 keeps characters, scenes and pacing consistent across extensions. It also admits room for improvement in complex motion and scenes with several people. Kling 4.0 Flash, the early-access version, stops at 20 seconds and 720p. For a 2-minute explainer, you&#x27;d plan scenes rather than stretch one clip. Our guide to long AI videos without stitching 8-second clips covers that. Which is better for 4K and big screens? Kling, on published specs. Kling 3.0 has made native 4K since April, and its API charges $0.42 a second at 4K. Seedance 2.0 also reaches 4K through BytePlus&#x27;s API, at an estimated $0.78 a second. Seedance 2.5 tops out at 1080p, though in 10-bit color. In Kling&#x27;s app, the 4K mode costs 30 credits a second. Kling&#x27;s plans page lists 1080p and 4K on paid plans only. Kling 4.0 adds 10-bit HDR at 1080p and 4K, which Kling marks as coming soon. Which follows references and keeps characters the same? Seedance 2.5 takes the most files: up to 30 images, 10 videos and 10 audio clips in one run. Kling 4.0 takes up to 15 files and 10 keyframes. Kling 3.0 Omni can build a character from a 3 to 8 second video, voice included. Kling&#x27;s Element Library saves a character once for reuse, which suits a recurring presenter or product. Seedance&#x27;s larger file count suits one complex scene with many parts. Which handles dialogue and other languages? Both make sound with the picture, including dialogue and lip sync. Seedance 2.5 lists 11 languages, including Arabic, Thai, Indonesian and Vietnamese. Kling 3.0 lists five, plus regional accents. Kling 4.0 adds Portuguese, German, French and Hindi, and accents such as Indian English. So the language can settle it. A German or Hindi ad points to Kling 4.0 once it&#x27;s live. An Arabic or Thai ad points to Seedance 2.5. For English, Spanish, Japanese or Korean, both list support, so test your own script. Which costs less per second? At 1080p on the makers&#x27; APIs, Kling costs less: $0.14 to $0.168 a second with audio. Seedance 2.0 costs about $0.37 and Seedance 2.5 about $0.57. At 720p, Seedance 2.0 Mini is the cheapest option at about $0.08 a second. For most ads with people in them, we&#x27;d start with Seedance 2.0, and move to 2.5 when the clip needs 20 or 30 seconds. We&#x27;d use Kling 3.0 for a 4K hero shot, or a scripted scene where every cut has to land where we planned it. A 15-second product or UGC ad: Seedance 2.0. Draft on 2.0 Fast or Mini, then render the keeper on 2.0. A dialogue scene: Seedance when the acting matters most. Kling when you need exact shot lengths and cut points. A 30-second story in one take: Seedance 2.5 today. We&#x27;ll look at Kling 4.0 again once it&#x27;s fully live. A 4K hero shot for a big screen: Kling 3.0. If we had to name one thing each does better: Seedance makes people move like people, whether they&#x27;re eating, fighting or skating. Kling does what the shot list says, cut for cut. Can I use Seedance and Kling for free? + Kling has a free Basic plan; its paid plans add 1080p and 4K, watermark removal and commercial use.
