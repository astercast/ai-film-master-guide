# Run the grade sheet

## Use when

At picture lock, before the creative grade — and at every grading session after, until the sheet reads clean and survives the scopes check.

## Inputs

- Scene list in cut order
- One hero reference shot per scene
- Source per scene (single model, or which models were mixed)
- The show look and the grain decision

## Procedure

1. Work the five pre-grade gates in order: every element assigned to plate or grade; hero reference chosen for every scene; one show look locked with scene variations only; grain philosophy decided (one grain, applied last, over everything); pre-grade masters archived.
2. Log every scene with its hero reference, its source, and its state today: unified, drifting, or unmatched.
3. Respect the mechanical rules: a scene with no hero reference reads unmatched whatever its owner claims — unification is matching to a reference; a scene claimed unified with matching steps unfinished reads drifting.
4. Work the matching steps in order: exposure and white balance, contrast curve, saturation (desaturate 10–20% — AI oversaturates by default), then the de-plasticize texture pass (fine grain, a whisper of chromatic aberration, soften, never sharpen), skin last and on a big screen.
5. Close with the chapter's QC: scopes, not eyes — blacks actually reach black, skin tones sit on the skin-tone line, no channel clips; broadcast-safe levels; the one grain over everything at the end.

## Worked example

Worked example on the sheet (grade.html): 'The Night Bus' across 5 scenes — 4 unified, the rain stop drifting because it was shot across two models and its saturation is unmatched — reads 'Hold the grade' until the stack is finished, then 'Grade clean'.

## Pitfalls

- Grading by eye — AI footage's built-in grade is designed to flatter on first glance; the scopes tell the truth.
- A new creative look per scene instead of one show look with variations.
- Torturing the grade to match two models that won't match — re-generate the outlier.
