# Run the script-clearance file

## What this gets you

Your script will be checked the way distributors' lawyers check it, names, places, brands, real people, with problems fixed before they can block release.

## Use when

From the final shooting script through delivery, clearance starts before production and the completed file is the gate for insurance (E&O) cover and distribution.

## Inputs

- The final shooting script
- The writer's annotation guide
- The production's item log (names, places, businesses, persons, locations, featured objects)

## Procedure

1. Commission the clearance report on the final shooting script: every character name, place, address and business researched, and the permission contact named for every referenced product, copyright and trademark (rule 1358).
2. Get the annotation guide from the writer: every character, place and event labelled fictional, real or composite; origins and any similar submission's independent path documented; true-life sources recorded as primary or secondary (rule 1359).
3. Close name coincidences before use, change or release any fictional name, business or address that matches a real, identifiable person or entity; only permitted telephone and plate numbers ever appear (rule 1360).
4. Prove the chain of title as an unbroken filed record, origin plus every transfer, work-for-hire or option tested, unreleased contributors found, encumbrances quitclaimed, and verify it with a copyright report covering domestic, foreign and renewal rights (rule 1361).
5. Research the title before final selection: the title report lists prior same and similar uses; a conflict changes the title (rule 1362).
6. Collect written releases: every recognizably depicted person (thinly disguised and deceased included, via representatives or heirs), every performer, recognizable extra and crew member, and every distinctive location (rule 1363).
7. Clear featured props, products, artwork and photographs one by one under an abundance of caution, never assume a supplied or previously used item is already cleared (rule 1364).
8. Keep the report living: update it whenever new material enters, script versions, boards, schedules, the shoot, post-production (rule 1365).
9. Close every flagged item with a recorded decision, license it, substitute it, change the use, or expressly assume the risk; an undecided flag blocks the element it flags (rule 1366).
10. Run the final check before exhibition: review the finished film against the file for what the script never contained, give trailers and publicity their own clearance analysis, and treat the completed file as the gate, no completed clearance, no E&O cover and no distribution (rule 1367).

## Worked example

Worked example on the clearance desk (clearance.html): an 8-item AI-short file with 5 flags, 4 closed by recorded decisions, 1 poster-artwork flag left open, reads 'Gate held' until the last flag is decided, then 'Gate open'.

## Pitfalls

- Treating clearance as a single pass at the start, new material enters at every stage and the report must follow it.
- Assuming a prop-house or previously used item is already cleared, the caution exists precisely because prominence in the final cut is unknowable in advance.
- Leaving a flagged item undecided and shipping the element anyway, the recorded-decision rule blocks it, and the E&O gate checks the completed file.
