# Run the take sheet

## What this gets you

You will have a working take sheet (the Take Sheet page) that logs every performance beat and tells you when the acting is ready to lock.

## Use when

In rehearsal, from the first 720p draft to Scene Lock — and at every rehearsal review after, until the sheet reads clean.

## Inputs

- Beat list in scene order with who performs each beat
- The director's read (shared event + a channel per character)
- Eyeline target per beat
- Framing plan with the emotional peaks marked

## Procedure

1. Work the five pre-lock gates in order: every beat rewritten as a playable tactic (direct, don't describe); eyelines named per beat; director's read written; identity locked (the face anchor is never re-run); restraint pass done (negative constraints, subtext, under-directed faces).
2. Log every beat with its direction — a playable tactic or an emotion label — its eyeline named or not, its dead-eyes check, its framing, whether it sits on an emotional peak, its failed rehearsals, and its route: in rehearsal, Scene-Locked, simplify, or hybrid.
3. Respect the mechanical rules: an emotion label reads undirected — models can't render an adjective, they can render a task; three failed rehearsals read overdue — simplify the shot, not the actor, and re-direct with a changed variable, never the same prompt hoping; a close-up away from its peak reads misspent — ration close-ups to the peaks.
4. Treat 720p drafts as rehearsal takes: does the tactic read, do the eyes have a job, does the turn land in the cut? The reaction shot is your best friend — the listener sells the line.
5. On approval, Scene-Lock the take: photograph the exact performance from new angles; the model stops reinterpreting dialogue, gestures, pauses, and props. If a scene's whole job is one complicated human feeling, assess it honestly for hybrid.

## Worked example

Worked example on the sheet (take.html): 'The Night Bus' across 6 beats — 5 Scene-Locked clean, the depot-office radio reaction still in rehearsal with one failed rehearsal — reads 'Hold performance lock' until the reaction locks, then 'Performance lock clean'.

## Pitfalls

- Describing emotion instead of directing it — 'she looks devastated' produces a stock expression; a playable tactic produces a performance.
- Fixing dead eyes with lighting — they're fixed by giving the eyes a job; if a performance feels dead, check the eyeline first.
- Re-rolling the same prompt after three failed rehearsals — the rule is simplify the shot, not the actor.
